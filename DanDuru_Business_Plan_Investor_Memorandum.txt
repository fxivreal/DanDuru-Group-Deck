1B. FLOURISHSCENT (FLOURISHCRAFT) - PERFUMERY SUBSIDIARY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

FlourishScent is the perfumery arm of DanDuru Group, operating under the
Flourishcraft brand. It is a Lagos-based distributor of fragrance oils and
perfume sprays. Undiluted perfume oil is scarce in Nigeria and West Africa;
FlourishScent buys it directly from importers who import and sell locally,
and resells it across retail and bulk/wholesale channels, with pack sizes
ranging from 3ml samples to 25kg bulk containers.
Our goal is to import undiluted perfume oil directly to secure supply and
control cost.

Principal Products:
- Perfume oils (retail and wholesale)
- Perfume sprays / mists
- Bulk fragrance oils (up to 25kg) for resellers, wholesalers and businesses
- Sample sizes (3ml) for retail demonstration and low-cost entry

Sales Channels:
- Retail: small packs (3ml - 100ml) for direct consumers
- Bulk/Wholesale: drums and containers (up to 25kg) supplied to resellers,
  retailers and other businesses for private labeling or re-packaging

Status: Already selling. FlourishScent is generating revenue today and operates
from Lagos. Current actual revenue: N2,500,000 per month. Projected
full-capacity revenue: N45,000,000 per month, blended across all SKUs (3ml
Sugar Baby, larger bottles, sprays, and 25kg bulk drums).
FlourishScent has 50+ returning customers with established retail partnerships and bulk supply agreements across Lagos.

 

Strategic Rationale for the Group:
- Diversification: food (volume, thin margin) balanced by fragrance (higher margin)
- Shared infrastructure: distribution network, finance, HR and management
  overhead are shared across both subsidiaries
- Complementary demand: both serve the Nigerian mass market and wholesale trade
- One capital, two engines: the same working capital facility funds raw material
  for both lines, with food providing volume and fragrance providing margin

4. INDUSTRY PROSPECTS & COMPETITION
================================================================================

In Nigeria, the market for consumer products has over time proved to be large
enough to accommodate even companies that do not have a good product. Due to the
large population and limited production activity, it has become assumed that the
market will always be there. Our assessment is that market growth in our chosen
business areas is upward and maturity may not be reached in the next ten years.
Two clear factors will enable continuous growth:

Firstly, due to increasing economic activity, fast urbanization and increasing
commercial activities, people are giving less attention to home cooking and
traditional food preparation. Fast food and food drinks have become staple food
among busy Nigerians.

Secondly, the current government direction towards encouraging local manufacturing
and restricting the importation of finished food products will further open the
market for locally produced beverages.

--- Cocoa Based and Milk Beverage Sector ---

The chocolate/cocoa based food drink sector was dominated by Cadbury, WAMCO and
Nestle. Promasidor Nigeria Ltd has gained significant market share. WAMCO plays
at the premium range with Three Crowns serving the medium tier. Promasidor
produces for the mass market.

The mass market cocoa based beverage market in Nigeria is estimated to be over
N50 billion.

The upper/premium segment comprising mainly Nestle Milo, Bournvita and Ovaltine
is estimated to be over N40 billion.

A review of product quality, packaging, pack sizes and prices of current market
leaders shows a significant opportunity for DanDuru to win market share. DanFam
Choco has been formulated and packaged to be very competitive in both quality
and pricing.

--- Sugar ---

Sugar consumption in Nigeria is currently estimated to be over 1.6 million tons
per annum but Nigeria produces only about 50,000 tons (approximately 4% of
national consumption). The huge balance is imported in raw form from Brazil by
Dangote and Bua Sugar Refineries.

The two big players in the Nigerian sugar business are Dangote and Bua Sugar
Refineries. JOF Limited and Josepdam and Sons Nigeria Ltd have approval to
import unfortified sugar and sell mainly to large beverage manufacturers.
Together with Golden Sugar, these refiners control roughly 95% of supply, but
almost entirely as bulk (50kg bags) sold to factories, beverage makers,
bakeries and confectioners. The National Sugar Development Council estimates
that less than 20% of consumption reaches households.

DanDuru operates downstream of these refiners. Our model is bulk breaking: we
buy refined sugar in bulk bags and repack it into hygienic, NAFDAC-approved,
vitamin-A-fortified retail packs — 15g single-serve sachets, plus 500g and 1kg
family packs — for the domestic market. Imported
refined sugar in retail packaging is banned by the Federal Government, so all
household sugar must be packaged in Nigeria — a structural advantage for
local packagers like DanDuru. The household retail segment is estimated at
approximately N50-60 billion annually.

St. Louis Cube, Golden Penny and Dangote Sugar are the major table sugar brands
positioned as premium brands in a non-competitive market.

Except for industrial users and bakers, granulated sugar products are mainly
purchased by the general public in single-serve packs from informal packers who
are not NAFDAC approved. Participation in this industry is now strictly regulated
by NAFDAC because of the Vitamin A fortification program.

--- Custard Powders ---

The custard powder market in Nigeria is fragmented with numerous uncertified
players. Credible competitors include Checker's Custard, Gold Custard and
Hollandia Custard. DanDuru has developed and introduced its range of custard
products bundled with milk and sugar (Milk Custard, 3-in-1) and another variant
bundled with milk, sugar and cocoa (Choco Custard, 4-in-1).

--- Chilli Pepper ---

Chilli pepper is a highly profitable venture in today's Nigerian market. It is
a staple ingredient in Nigerian cuisine, particularly in popular dishes like
suya, jollof rice, and stews. Nigeria relies heavily on imports to meet demand,
creating an opportunity for local producers.

With the right investment, farming practices, and marketing strategies, chilli
pepper production and trade can be a lucrative business in Nigeria.

--- Perfume & Fragrance Sector ---

The Nigerian fragrance market is substantial and growing, driven by a young,
fashion-conscious population and a strong culture of fragrance use across all
income levels. Undiluted perfume oil is scarce in Nigeria and West Africa as a
whole; most of what is available comes through importers who import and sell
locally. This creates a clear opportunity for brands that blend and package
quality products from scarce undiluted oils at accessible prices.

The market spans:
- Mass market perfumed oils and body sprays - high volume, frequent purchase
- Mid-tier branded perfumes - aspirational consumers
- Bulk/wholesale supply of fragrance oils - resellers, retailers, event
  businesses and private-label brands

FlourishScent's model is direct sourcing of imported raw materials:
buying scarce undiluted perfume oil from importers today, and importing
directly to secure supply and cut cost, before reselling locally. This gives
faster restocking, quality assurance and the ability to offer both retail
packs and bulk quantities (3ml to 25kg) in one business.

Premium fragrances in Nigeria were worth approximately USD 326.8 million (~N490 billion) in 2025
(Deep Market Insights); with mass-market oils and sprays added, the combined market is well above
N500 billion. The category is import-dominated and fragmented (Euromonitor: leading brand Oriflame
holds ~7% share), so value accrues to brands that blend and package locally. Nigeria also exports
fragrances to West and Central Africa (DRC, Tanzania), which FlourishScent's export ambitions can
piggyback on.

7. THE GAP ANALYSIS
================================================================================

DanDuru Group has identified the following gaps in the market:

- Packaged sugar for domestic consumption: The unfulfilled market for hygienically
  packaged granulated sugar is a clear gap. DanDuru's packaged granulated sugar
  is a response to consumers' need for hygienically distributed sugar.

- Ready-to-drink beverages: Current big players have concentrated on powder and
  condensed products. DanDuru intends to introduce ready-to-drink beverages.

- Rock-bottom pricing: Due to the growing nature of the industry, prices remain
  high. DanDuru shall fill the gap of low-price strategy.

- Distribution network: The DanDuru distribution network will ensure retailers
  and consumers have constant access to our products.

- Contract manufacturing: As restrictions on importation of finished products
  continue, DanDuru will produce for Nigerian traders, giving each customer a
  different product to market.

- Hospitality industry: Currently sources sugar products from abroad. DanDuru
  is poised to meet that need locally.

- Affordable local fragrance: The Nigerian perfume market is dominated by
  imported brands priced beyond the mass market. FlourishScent fills the gap of
  quality fragrance oils and sprays sourced direct from importers at accessible
  prices, sold retail and in bulk to the wholesale trade.

- Bulk fragrance supply: Undiluted perfume oil is scarce in Nigeria and West
  Africa. FlourishScent buys it directly from importers and resells it,
  supplying resellers and small businesses from 3ml up to 25kg — a gap
  currently served by imports.

12. PRICING STRATEGY & MARGIN JUSTIFICATION
================================================================================

Our sachet price of N34 (15g) equates to approximately N2,267/kg, which is at a
premium to bulk retail sugar (N1,700-N2,000/kg). This premium is justified by:

- Hygienic, NAFDAC-approved packaging - unlike open-market "mallam" sugar sold
  in unmarked polythene
- Vitamin A fortification - mandated by law but not practiced by informal packers
- Single-serve convenience - 144 sachets per pack, targeting canteens, bukas,
  and low-income households who buy in small quantities daily
- Home-pack economy - 500g and 1kg packs serve families at per-kg prices closer
  to bulk, with the same hygiene and fortification guarantees
- Brand trust and traceability - consumers can identify and trust the DanFam brand

The 4.6% net margin on sugar is deliberately thin to drive volume. Higher-margin
products (chilli pepper at approximately 16% margin, chocolate powder, custard)
improve the blended profitability of the overall business.

FlourishScent pricing spans the 3ml sample pack (low entry price for retail
demonstration) up to 25kg bulk drums (volume pricing for wholesalers and
resellers). The blended gross margin across all SKUs is estimated at 38.8%,
based on the flagship 3ml Sugar Baby actual — N400 selling price, N245 cost
(oil N147 + bottle N88 + sticker N10) — giving the Group a higher-margin
earnings engine alongside the volume-driven food business. Because the mix may
shift toward lower-margin bulk drums as the line scales, we also model a
conservative 30% blended-margin scenario; at that level the line still nets
N13,100,000/month (N157.2M annually).
